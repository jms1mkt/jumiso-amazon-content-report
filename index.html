Real Seller Central data at daily resolution (all 158 days) shows page views and revenue moving together very closely overall (r = 0.93) — but that number is almost entirely a promo-day effect. Strip out the four promo windows and the day-to-day relationship all but disappears (r = 0.18 on the 118 ordinary days): normal daily swings in page views don’t reliably predict that day's revenue, they're just noisy together. Content matched to this specific product tells a similarly mixed story at daily grain: the two highest-reach videos of the period (@rw2100, 134k views; @lifeofshauni, 78k views) landed on ordinary days with only a modest revenue bump, while @_leaveitblankk's 8/14 post (42k views, 10.63% ER — the best engagement of any tracked post) landed on a promo day, so its lift can’t be cleanly separated from the discount. Elio's 8/27 post (flagged live in Slack) can’t be verified yet — Seller Central data lags 1–2 days and 8/27–28 aren’t available as of this report (checked again same day, still blank).

Key insights

  • At daily resolution, the views↔revenue correlation is almost entirely a promo-day effect. Across all 158 days, r = 0.93 — but on the 118 days with no promotion running, that drops to r = 0.18. Day-to-day, ordinary page-view swings don't reliably forecast that day's revenue; the strong overall number is being driven by four short, huge promo windows, not a steady daily relationship. (Dropping only Prime Day + the Aug Best Deal, and keeping the smaller Big Spring Sale / Best Deal promos in, gives r = 0.74 — still mostly a promo story.)
  • Prime Day remains the single biggest revenue event of the period. Jun 23 hit $27,487 in real revenue in one day (+539% page views, +914% revenue vs. the day before) with no confirmed content upload that day, then fell back −79% four days later — a textbook promo spike-and-reversion, not a content effect.
  • The clearest content/promo overlap is Aug 14. @_leaveitblankk's post (42,100 views, 10.63% ER — the best engagement of any tracked USA post) landed on a day with $8,455 in real revenue (well above the ~$2,500 non-promo daily baseline) — but the Aug Best Deal (40% off) was active that same day, so the lift can’t be cleanly credited to the post alone.
  • Raw view count doesn’t predict a revenue bump — engagement rate and timing do. @rw2100 (134,475 views) and @lifeofshauni (77,600 views) both landed on ordinary, non-promo revenue days ($2,015–2,560, in line with the ~$2,500 baseline). The best real evidence of an organic lift is @abigailcanfieldd's 7/2 post (13,100 views, 5.56% ER, revenue +25% same-day) — decent reach and decent engagement, together, on a non-promo day.
  • Elio's 8/27 post still can’t be verified. Seller Central's own reporting lags 1–2 days; 8/27–28 were checked again the same day as this report and are still blank (8/26 is the latest confirmed day: $2,398 revenue, in line with the recent declining trend, no anomaly). The live jump you saw in the Seller Central dashboard is real-time and more precise than this daily report will ever show; once 8/27–28 data lands here, it can be added to confirm the size of the bump against the days before.
  • A tracker data-quality fix surfaced 349 previously-hidden real posts. 406 rows were logged under two placeholder batch dates instead of real ones; checking each post's actual Instagram/TikTok timestamp via Apify and correcting the sheet revealed a staggered "Picky" rollout — a Gifted March cohort (US_Picky_Mar, 293 posts) and a Paid April cohort (US_Picky_Apr, 109 posts, including the 53-post peak on 5/10) — mostly Apr 25–May 25, that the report previously undercounted as 34 dated posts total. Despite the much larger real volume, it shows no standout revenue signal beyond the Best Deal promo it partly overlaps — reinforcing insight #4: raw post volume doesn't move revenue on its own.
  • Excluding the Picky program entirely still shows no post↔revenue correlation. Dropping all 402 Picky posts (Gifted and Paid alike) leaves 65 posts across 35 dates — the genuine one-off influencer content. Same-day post count vs. revenue on non-promo days: r = −0.08 (r = −0.02 across the full window, r = −0.01 using next-day revenue instead of same-day). Non-promo days with a post average $2,529 in revenue vs. $2,586 on days without one — essentially no difference. Restricting further to only Paid-collab posts (34 dates, still includes US_Picky_Apr) gives the same result (r = −0.08 same-day). At this sample size these small negative r values read as noise, not a real effect — the honest conclusion is that individual influencer posts, paid or gifted, don't produce a detectable daily revenue signal independent of promotions.
